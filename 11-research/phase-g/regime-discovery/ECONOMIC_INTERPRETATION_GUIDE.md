# Economic Interpretation Guide

## Bull Trend Expansion

- **Rationale:** Gold trends most cleanly when persistence, liquidity support, and macro pressure align in the same direction.
- **Mechanics:** Breakouts are reinforced rather than faded, and validation quality improves because directional structure dominates noise.
- **Participants:** Trend followers, macro allocators, momentum-driven discretionary flows
- **Backdrop:** Supportive USD and rates backdrop with persistent upside structure
- **Liquidity:** Orderly and supportive
- **Volatility:** Moderate and controlled
- **Transition triggers:** sustained breakout, positive macro pressure, stable volatility
- **Failure modes:** late breakout exhaustion, macro shock reversal

## Bear Trend Unwind

- **Rationale:** Weak persistent structure forces gold research to distinguish between orderly unwind and true crisis behavior.
- **Mechanics:** Negative trend persistence remains present, but not every downtrend carries the same validation penalty.
- **Participants:** Systematic trend followers, directional macro sellers, hedgers
- **Backdrop:** USD firmness or rates pressure with fading gold demand
- **Liquidity:** Adequate but one-sided
- **Volatility:** Below crisis but directionally persistent
- **Transition triggers:** breakdown persistence, negative trend gap, fading macro support
- **Failure modes:** violent reversal, event-driven squeeze

## Calm Carry Accumulation

- **Rationale:** Low-volatility environments compress noise and allow slow information accumulation without crisis dislocation.
- **Mechanics:** Signals persist longer, but payoff asymmetry is smaller than in true bull trend expansion.
- **Participants:** Carry allocators, reserve managers, low-turnover macro funds
- **Backdrop:** Calm policy environment and muted USD/rates shocks
- **Liquidity:** Deep and orderly
- **Volatility:** Low
- **Transition triggers:** volatility compression, absence of major event stress, stable trend backdrop
- **Failure modes:** false calm before event shock, range stagnation

## Crisis Dislocation

- **Rationale:** Crisis periods create large moves but poor research reliability unless treated as a separate state.
- **Mechanics:** Volatility dominates signal quality, and contradictions increase because edge persistence degrades.
- **Participants:** Forced de-risking, macro hedgers, crisis liquidity takers
- **Backdrop:** Stress episodes, geopolitical escalation, or policy disorder
- **Liquidity:** Fragmented and shock-prone
- **Volatility:** High and unstable
- **Transition triggers:** volatility spike, geopolitical activation, behavioral stretch extremes
- **Failure modes:** signal inversion, execution instability

## Macro Policy Transition

- **Rationale:** Policy and cross-asset shocks change the information content of gold faster than trend-only taxonomies capture.
- **Mechanics:** Macro pressure and event stress alter the ranking of candidate hypotheses and explain why macro-only signals fail when isolated.
- **Participants:** Macro funds, discretionary central-bank watchers, event-driven allocators
- **Backdrop:** Policy repricing, real-yield shifts, DXY transitions
- **Liquidity:** Tradable but sensitive to event timing
- **Volatility:** Moderate with event spikes
- **Transition triggers:** macro pressure extremes, policy surprise, event density
- **Failure modes:** false event follow-through, short-lived narrative shift

## Range Compression

- **Rationale:** Most research time is spent outside explicit crisis or event transitions, so a broad compression state is required as the institutional default.
- **Mechanics:** Signals are weaker than in bull trend expansion but more reliable than in crisis dislocation.
- **Participants:** Balanced macro books, range traders, hedging flows
- **Backdrop:** No dominant macro shock and no clean breakout persistence
- **Liquidity:** Normal
- **Volatility:** Moderate
- **Transition triggers:** absence of extreme macro pressure, trend ambiguity, mid-range volatility
- **Failure modes:** hidden transition starting inside the range, feature redundancy
